Hidden in plain sight

Three ways to make a LatticeWire tunnel look like the rest of the web, from a QUIC handshake of your own to the genuine handshake of a real site.

What the network sees

Pick a mode and watch the capture an observer would record.

    A fresh connection every half hour

    Stealth clients move to a new cover connection, with a new port and new connection IDs, at a random point around the interval you set. The tunnel inside carries on without a dropped ping.

    One key per device

    Give every client its own stealth key in its config and in its entry on the server. A lost or retired device is revoked by changing that one entry; every other device keeps working, and connection IDs carry nothing that ties packets to a key.

    The best cover is your own site

    Use a domain you control, so the site named in the handshake really lives at your server's address.

    1. Point a domain at your server

      For example cdn.example.net.

    2. Run your site on other ports

      LatticeWire takes UDP and TCP 443. Serve the site with HTTPS and HTTP/3 on 8443 and plain HTTP on 8080, for example with Caddy.

    3. Point LatticeWire at it

      stealth_mode  = "ghost"
mask_sni      = "cdn.example.net"
mask_server   = "127.0.0.1:8443"
tcp_listen    = "0.0.0.0:443"
cover_backend = "127.0.0.1:8080"

    4. Connect clients with transport = "auto"

      They use QUIC when they can and TLS on 443 when they can't. Visitors who aren't clients see your website on both.
